# Venue shortlist (M19 S1): where to submit V-cloak

Date checked: **2026-10-13** (session 46). Every date below was read on the venue's own site on that day unless
the row says otherwise. Deadlines move: check the URL again before you commit to one.

Criterion 9 of Review 4 gives marks only for **documentary proof of a submission** (confirmation email as a PDF,
the submission or paper ID, a screenshot of the portal). Acceptance is not required. So the two questions that
decide the route are: does the deadline fall before Review 4, and is a receipt issued on the day of submission.

**Not known to the agent: the date of Review 4.** Every "in time?" judgement depends on it. Tell the agent.

## 1. The three routes

| | (a) International conference | (b) Peer-reviewed journal | (c) Patent filing through the institute's IP cell |
|---|---|---|---|
| Deadline | fixed, see section 2 | none (rolling) | none (the IP cell has its own queue: ask) |
| Fee to submit | none at the venues below | none to submit; a fee on acceptance at an open-access journal | a filing fee under Schedule I of the Patents Rules |
| Proof on the day | receipt from the submission system | receipt from the submission system | "an acknowledgement receipt will be generated" after payment (ipindia.gov.in/filing-process) |
| Who presses submit | one author with an account | the corresponding author | the IP cell or its attorney, not the team |
| Work for the agent | fit the paper to the template and page limit, anonymise | fit the paper to the journal template | a different document: claims and a specification, not the paper |
| Main risk | missing the date; a 12-page limit | a fee of about 2,000 USD if accepted (IEEE Access) | a public disclosure before filing can destroy novelty; the timing is not in the team's hands |

One manuscript goes to **one** venue at a time: every venue below forbids a parallel submission. A patent filing
and a paper submission can coexist, but ask the IP cell first, because the order matters (file, then publish).

### Notes on what could not be verified
- **A. PoPETs page limit and template.** The 12-page limit and the template rule were read on
  https://petsymposium.org/authors.php, which says its guidance applies to PoPETs 2023 and 2024. The 2027 call
  points to "the submission guidelines" without restating the limit. Read the current guidelines before S2.
- **B. Receipts.** Options 1 to 3 use HotCRP-style submission servers and option 5 uses the IEEE author portal.
  None of the venue pages states that a confirmation email is sent on submission. That such systems send one is
  the normal behaviour, not something read on these pages. Before the last day: register the paper early and
  confirm that the email arrives. The patent route is the only one whose own site promises a receipt.
- **C. Patent fee.** The First Schedule PDF could not be opened from this machine (HTTP 404 on the indexed link).
  A search summary gave 1,600 INR for e-filing by an educational institution; that figure is **not verified**
  on the official site and must not be quoted. The IP cell will know the fee and who pays it.
- **D. IEEE Access first decision.** The site gives the submission-to-publication target only, not a time to the
  first decision, and does not say in words that submission is open all year (there is no deadline on the page).

## 4. Fit of the paper to each option (the agent's reading, not a fact about the venues)

- **Scope.** V-cloak hides the order of stored messages from the server: a metadata-privacy system. That is the
  stated subject of PoPETs, and inside the scope of ASIACCS and ACNS (applied security systems).
- **What the reviewers will see.** The evidence has a negative result (dudect FAIL rows for the AEAD stages and the
  whole call, ADR-023), kernels claimed oblivious only at the kernel level, several NOT_MEASURED items, timings on
  one shared cloud T4 with UNSTABLE rows, and no end-to-end numbers yet (M15 is NOT_STARTED). Options 1 to 4 are
  selective venues; a rejection there is likely enough that nobody should count on an acceptance. Criterion 9 does
  not need one.
- **Length.** The size of the current Overleaf paper is not in the repository, so the agent cannot say how much
  must be cut for a 12-page limit. ACNS (20 pages, LNCS single column) and IEEE Access (no limit) are the roomy ones.
- **Anonymity.** Options 1 to 4 are anonymous: the guide's name, the institute and the repository link come off
  the PDF, and the author block of S4 goes into the submission form instead. IEEE Access shows the authors.
- **Cost if accepted.** PoPETs: none stated. ASIACCS: registration plus a possible 500 to 750 USD. ACNS: travel to
  the UK is mandatory. IEEE Access: 2,160 USD.
- **Calendar.** With today at 13 October 2026, option 1 leaves about seven weeks for S2 to S5, including the
  guide's approval (S4). Option 5 and option 6 have no date of their own and can be done as soon as S4 closes.

## 5. What the agent needs from the humans (S1 decision)

1. The **date of Review 4** (and whether the panel accepts "submitted, under review" as proof, which the rubric

   SCI indexing required by the institute). The rubric says "peer-reviewed journal / international conference":
   if the institute wants a specific index, say so before S2.
4. For route (c): the contact and the procedure of the institute's IP cell.
